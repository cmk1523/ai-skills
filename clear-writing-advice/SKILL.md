# Clear writing advice

Revise or draft the user's text so a reader can see who acts, what that person must do, and when the rule applies. Follow the [Principles of Clear Writing](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/write/legal-docs/clear-writing.html) from the Office of the Federal Register. Those principles adapt the Federal Plain Language Guidelines for regulations.

Preserve every duty, prohibition, permission, condition, exception, number, citation, defined term, and fact. Change wording and structure. Keep the legal meaning.

## When to use

- The user asks to write clearly, use plain language, simplify, or rewrite for clarity.
- The user asks to draft or revise a regulation, policy, procedure, instruction, or other formal text.
- The user names this skill.

## Steps

1. Read the whole text. Identify the audience, each actor, each duty, and each condition.
2. Revise one principle at a time, in the order below.
3. Reread the draft against the checklist. Fix anything a later item still catches.
4. When a principle would change who is obligated, what is required, or when it applies, keep the original meaning and apply the principle only in a form that preserves it.
5. Do not invent an actor, duty, condition, or exception. When a duty has no actor and the text does not make the actor obvious, ask who must act.

If the user gives no text and does not ask for a draft, ask for the text or the drafting task before you write.

## Principles

### Name the actor

Write in the active voice. Put the actor before the verb.

Passive: The material will be delivered.

Active: The messenger will deliver the material.

Use the passive voice only when the actor is unknown, unimportant, or obvious. In regulatory and procedural text, that case is rare.

### Use an action verb

Replace a noun that hides a verb with the verb.

| Don't say | Say |
| --- | --- |
| give consideration to | consider |
| is applicable to | applies to |
| make payment | pay |
| give recognition to | recognize |
| is concerned with | concerns |

### Use must for a duty

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| must | Imposes a duty |
| must not | Prohibits an action |
| will | Predicts a future action |
| should | Recommends an action that is not an absolute duty |
| may | Grants discretion |
| shall | Avoid. Readers may read it as a prediction |

Don't say: The Governor shall approve it.

Say: The Governor must approve it. (a duty)

Or: The Governor will approve it. (a future action)

Map each modal already in the text to the row that matches its meaning, then use that row's word. Do not change a duty into a recommendation, or a recommendation into a duty.

### Address the reader

Use the imperative for procedures, how-to steps, and lists of duties.

Sign all copies.

Attach a copy of your W-2 to your return.

### Use the present tense

A rule of continuing effect speaks at the time the reader applies it.

Don't say: The fine for driving without a license shall be $10.

Say: The fine for driving without a license is $10.

Use will only to predict a future action.

### State the point positively

When a positive sentence can carry the same meaning, write it that way.

Don't say: The Governor may not appoint persons other than those qualified by the Personnel Management Agency.

Say: The Governor must appoint a person qualified by the Personnel Management Agency.

Use a negative to caution the reader: Don't run. Don't smoke.

Do not stack negatives in one sentence.

Don't say: A demonstration project will not be approved unless the applicant meets all requirements.

Say: A demonstration project will be approved only if the applicant meets all requirements.

| Don't say | Say |
| --- | --- |
| not honest | dishonest |
| did not remember | forgot |
| did not pay any attention to | ignored |
| did not remain at the meeting | left the meeting |
| did not comply with | violated |

Keep a negative when a positive rewrite would add or remove a duty.

### Name the category, then any exception

State who the rule covers. Do not define the group by listing who it excludes.

Don't say: All persons except those 18 years or older must...

Say: Each person under 18 years of age must...

Use an exception when it replaces a long list. Name the category first, then the exception.

Say: Each state except Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona must ration...

Keep every exception that changes who is covered.

### Keep the infinitive together

Don't say: Be sure to promptly reply to the invitation.

Say: Be sure to reply promptly to the invitation.

### Use the singular when the duty is separate

A singular noun shows that the rule applies to each member of a class.

Don't say: The guard will issue security badges to the employees who work in Building D and Building E.

Say: The guard will issue a security badge to each employee who works in Building D and each employee who works in Building E.

Use the plural, or say the duty is joint, when the people act together or receive one shared thing. Do not switch number in a way that turns a joint duty into separate duties, or the reverse.

### Use one word for one thing

Repeat the precise term. Do not swap in a synonym.

Don't say: Each motor vehicle owner must register his or her car with the Automobile Division.

Say: Each automobile owner must register the automobile with the Automobile Division.

Do not use one word for two different things.

Don't say: The tank had a 200-gallon tank for fuel.

Say: The tank had a 200-gallon fuel container.

Copy a defined term as defined. Do not replace it with a simpler word.

### Make parallel ideas look parallel

Use the same grammatical form for items that have the same role, especially in a list.

Nonparallel:

- To take minutes of all the meetings
- The Executive Secretary answers all the correspondence
- Writing of monthly reports

Parallel:

- To take minutes of all the meetings
- To answer all the correspondence
- To write the monthly reports

### Prefer a simple word

Use the simple word when it has the same meaning. Keep a term of art when the simple word would change the meaning.

| Don't say | Say |
| --- | --- |
| construct, fabricate | make |
| initiate, commence | begin |
| terminate | end |
| utilize | use |
| substantial portion | large part |
| afforded an opportunity | allow |

### Cut needless words

| Don't say | Say |
| --- | --- |
| because of the fact that | because |
| call your attention to the fact that | remind you |
| for the period of | for |
| in many cases | often |
| in many instances | sometimes |
| in the nature of | like |
| the fact that he had not succeeded | his failure |
| the question as to whether | whether |

### Drop a redundant pair

Do not pair words that mean the same thing, or where one word already includes the other:

- any and all
- authorize and direct
- cease and desist
- each and every
- full and complete
- order and direct
- means and includes
- necessary and desirable

Keep a pair that is itself a term of art the user must preserve, such as a statutory "cease and desist."

### Name the concrete thing

Use the specific word you mean. Keep the broader word when the text covers the broader class.

| Broader word | Use the specific word when you mean |
| --- | --- |
| vehicles | automobiles |
| firearms | rifles |
| aircraft | helicopters |

### Choose words the reader does not resent

Prefer ability, achieve, benefit, guarantee, please, reasonable, reliable, service, useful, and you.

When another accurate word exists, avoid alibi, allege, blame, complaint, impossible, liable, oversight, unfortunate, waste, and wrong.

Keep the accurate word when a milder word would hide the fact.

### Break a noun string

Don't say: Underground mine worker safety protection procedures development.

Say: Development of procedures to protect the safety of workers in underground mines.

### Avoid a gendered term

Use singular "they" when you need a pronoun for a person whose gender is unspecified. Use a neutral job title.

| Don't say | Say |
| --- | --- |
| crewman | crewmember |
| draftsman | drafter |
| enlisted men | enlisted personnel |
| fireman | firefighter |
| foreman | supervisor |
| manhours | hours worked |
| manpower | personnel, or workforce |

Don't say: The administrator or his designee must complete the evaluation form.

Say: The administrator or the administrator's designee must complete the evaluation form.

Check the rewrite still means the same thing. "Each director will announce his or her recommendations" is a different statement from "The directors will announce their recommendation."

### Write a short sentence

Prefer a sentence that is simple, active, affirmative, and declarative. State one point in each sentence.

- Put one or two simple conditions before the rule.
- Put the rule before two or more complex conditions.
- Use a list when several conditions or subordinate provisions apply.

### Make the list match its introduction

- Use parallel structure.
- Each item, read with the introduction, is a complete thought.
- When the introduction is a complete sentence, end it with a colon and make each item its own sentence.
- When the introduction is an incomplete sentence in regulatory or formal legal text, end it with a dash. End each item except the last with a semicolon. After the semicolon on the next-to-last item, write "and" or "or". End the last item with a period.
- When you revise ordinary prose, keep a markdown list. Keep the items parallel, and make each item a complete thought with the introduction. Use "and" or "or" so the list states whether every item applies or any item applies.

### Keep the paragraph short

Give each paragraph one topic. Split a long technical discussion into a series of short paragraphs.

## Checklist

Review the draft once for each item:

- [ ] Each sentence names the actor and uses an active verb, unless the actor is unknown or unimportant.
- [ ] A duty uses must or must not. A prediction uses will. Discretion uses may. A recommendation uses should.
- [ ] Verbs stay verbs.
- [ ] A procedure speaks to the reader in the imperative.
- [ ] A continuing rule uses the present tense.
- [ ] A positive sentence carries the meaning wherever that stays accurate.
- [ ] Each category is stated directly. Any exception follows the category and still covers the same cases.
- [ ] Infinitives stay together.
- [ ] Singular and plural match whether the duty is separate or joint.
- [ ] One term stands for one thing, and each defined term is unchanged.
- [ ] List items are parallel, and each item completes the introduction.
- [ ] Simple, concrete words replace long or abstract ones where the meaning stays the same.
- [ ] Needless words and redundant pairs are gone.
- [ ] Noun strings are broken up.
- [ ] Job titles and pronouns are not gender-specific, and the meaning is unchanged.
- [ ] Each sentence and each paragraph states one point.

## Output

Return the revised text. Preserve headings, lists, tables, defined terms, citations, and numbering unless a principle requires a structural change.

When the user asks for advice, a review, or an explanation, name each principle you applied and show the before-and-after for each change. When the user asks only for a rewrite or a draft, return the revised text without a preamble.

Do not write the revision to disk unless the user asks you to save a file.

## Example

Input:

> The contractor shall give consideration to the question as to whether each motor vehicle owner will be afforded an opportunity to register his or her car with the Automobile Division. The administrator must approve the figures. All persons except those 18 years or older must not utilize the facility.

Output:

The contractor must consider whether each automobile owner may register the automobile with the Automobile Division. The administrator must approve the figures. Each person under 18 years of age must not use the facility.

That revision uses must for the duty, may for the permission, one term (automobile) for one thing, a direct category instead of an exception, and use instead of utilize. The prohibition stays a prohibition.

When the actor is missing, ask instead of naming one. For "The figures must be approved," ask who must approve the figures.
